By Staff Correspondent, Mussoorie

Mussoorie is in shock after a horrific robbery at the residence of Brigadier Ranjit Thakur (Retd.) in Landour claimed three lives on the night of December [redacted].

Background Research — The Bisht Family vs. The Thakurs

CBI/EX/019
Source: Municipal records, court filings, newspaper archives • Compiled by CBI research wing
CBI Intelligence Brief — Bisht-Thakur History

Harish Bisht (father of Rajveer Bisht) operated the Bisht Lodge near Landour Bazaar for 18 years. A modest but well-regarded establishment. In 2010, a land boundary dispute arose between the Bisht Lodge property and the adjacent Thakur estate.


Brigadier Ranjit Thakur, leveraging his military connections and relationship with the then-District Magistrate, escalated the dispute aggressively. Municipal records show that Bisht Lodge's operating licence was revoked on grounds of "encroachment on government land" — a claim that multiple contemporaneous assessments described as "technically dubious."


Harish Bisht filed two appeals. Both were dismissed. The lodge was shuttered. Financial records show Harish Bisht was bankrupt within three months.


Harish Bisht died on [date], 2011 — six months after losing the lodge. Cause of death: cardiac arrest. He was 54 years old. No prior history of heart disease.


His son, Rajveer Bisht, was 22 at the time. He did not contest the land dispute further. He left Mussoorie briefly, then returned and began a small property dealing business. Within five years, he had acquired significant land holdings in the Landour area. Today he is the owner of the Royal Pines Hotel and Mussoorie's largest private property portfolio.


NOTE: The Thakur family was killed approximately 7 months after Harish Bisht's death. Rajveer Bisht began acquiring Thakur-adjacent land approximately 5 months after the family's death.

CBI Note: Timeline is damning. The Brigadier destroys Bisht's father → father dies → 7 months later the Brigadier's entire family is killed → 5 months after that, the son starts buying their land. Revenge AND profit. The oldest motives in the world.
