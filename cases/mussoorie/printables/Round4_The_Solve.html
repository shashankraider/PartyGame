CBI/EX/023

CBI case file · document · Round 4

Post-Mortem — Independent CBI Medical Review

Independent review of Vikram's body identifies a blunt-force injury to the back of the head consistent with a lathi strike — not with a fall.

An independent post-mortem review commissioned by the CBI. The local autopsy report (which Inspector Devraj had received without comment) had listed cause of death as 'multiple blunt-force trauma consistent with a fall'. The independent review confirms most injuries are consistent with a fall — but isolates one specific injury at the base of the skull as 'a single, localised, cylindrical blunt-force impact, antemortem, inconsistent with surface impact during a fall'. The shape and dimensions are consistent with a service lathi.

Duty Register — Original Entry and Alteration Audit

MURDER IN MUSSOORIE / EVIDENCE REPORT

The entry was replaced

ORIGINAL / 8:05 PM

Left station on round.

Inspector Devraj Khanna

Departure recorded. Route not independently verified.
REPLACEMENT / EDITED AT 8:34 PM

Present at station throughout.

8:00-8:30 PM - Inspector Devraj Khanna; no departure.

Both versions survive in the district-held audit copy.
EDITOR ATTRIBUTIONDevraj's authenticated account, corroborated by desk CCTV showing him making the revision after returning.
The audit establishes an alteration of the whereabouts record. It does not by itself establish the motive for the edit or prove murder.
Full record and limitations

CBI comparison of the station’s electronic duty register, its district-held append-only audit copy and the station desk CCTV. The original entry at 8:05 PM on the murder night reads: “Inspector Devraj Khanna — left station on round.” At 8:34 PM, Devraj’s authenticated account replaced it with: “8:00–8:30 PM — Inspector Devraj Khanna present at station throughout; no departure.” The district copy preserves both versions and the edit time. The desk CCTV shows Devraj at that terminal making the revision after his return; it corroborates the account attribution rather than relying on a username alone. The CCTV does not show the assault. The preserved departure entry records a claimed round, not a verified route, and the replacement contradicts the earlier departure and the road CCTV. This establishes a deliberate alteration of his whereabouts record, not by itself the reason for it, a murder or the words of Bisht’s call.

Forensic Examination — Devraj’s Issued Lathi

MURDER IN MUSSOORIE / EVIDENCE REPORT

An identified service lathi

ISSUE RECORDAssigned to Inspector Devraj Khanna.
RECOVERYHis locked station equipment cabinet; sealing and transfers documented.
BLOOD / DNASingle-source profile matching Vikram Singh, recovered from the striking end.
INJURY COMPARISONCompatible dimensions and cylindrical shape. No unique wound impression identifying only this weapon.
The DNA link does not date the deposit, identify the wielder, establish the transfer mechanism or demonstrate a push.
Full record and limitations

Combined CBI seizure record, police equipment issue record and laboratory examination. The equipment record assigns service lathi DK-17 to Inspector Devraj Khanna. Investigators recovered that marked lathi from his locked equipment cabinet at the station and documented its sealing, transfers and receipt by the laboratory. Blood recovered from its striking end yielded a single-source DNA profile matching Vikram Singh’s reference sample. The laboratory records satisfactory controls and no indication of contamination in those controls. Comparison with the independent medical review finds the pre-fall head injury consistent with the seized lathi’s dimensions and cylindrical shape; the wound is not a unique impression identifying this weapon to the exclusion of all others. The DNA finding connects Vikram’s biological material to this particular recovered lathi. It does not date the deposit, identify who wielded it, establish the mechanism of transfer or show a push.
